What Is The Brain Song?

The Brain Song is a 12-minute digital audio program that uses layered sound patterns designed to gently guide the brain toward Gamma-frequency activity. The official page links Gamma brainwaves to BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor), a protein that neuroscientists often call "fertilizer for the brain" because of its role in communication between brain cells, learning and memory.

It is not a pill, supplement or device. You buy it, download it and press play.

How Does It Work?

The Brain Song relies on brainwave entrainment: the idea that a rhythmic sound can encourage brain activity to synchronize with that rhythm. The marketing focuses on Gamma activity (roughly 30–100 Hz, often discussed around 40 Hz) and says that Gamma "may play a role" in encouraging BDNF activity. The routine is simple: put on headphones, sit quietly for about 12 minutes, once a day.

What Does the Science Actually Say?

Real: BDNF and Gamma oscillations are legitimate research topics, and some studies explore 40 Hz sensory stimulation in the lab.

Not proven: We found no peer-reviewed or independent clinical trial showing that this specific audio raises your BDNF or improves memory or focus. Several independent reviewers make the same point: general neuroscience does not automatically validate a commercial track. Note also that the official site states its claims have not been evaluated by the FDA and that the product does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.
